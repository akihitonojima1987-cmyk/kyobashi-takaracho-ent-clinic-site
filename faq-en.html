Fees and insurance

QCan travelers or people without Japanese health insurance see a doctor?

Yes. Japanese public health insurance cannot be used, so you pay the full cost yourself (all prices include tax).

  • Consultation and simple treatment: 16,500 yen
  • Consultation + one test: 22,000 yen
  • Consultation + detailed tests (hearing test, blood test, etc.): 33,000 yen
  • Consultation + minor surgery (e.g. myringotomy): 55,000 yen
  • Follow-up visit (within 7 days, same symptoms): 11,000 yen
  • Continuing B-spot therapy (2nd visit or later, treatment only, within 1 month of the previous one): 7,700 yen (About B-spot therapy)
  • English medical certificate for travel insurance: +11,000 yen

Medicine from the pharmacy is also charged in full, separately.

Consultations are in Japanese. If speaking Japanese is difficult for you, please come with an interpreter. Without an interpreter, we may not be able to see you.

If you would like a consultation using a translation app, the translation fee is included in the fees above (there is no separate charge).

QI have Japanese health insurance, but speaking Japanese is difficult. Can I see a doctor?

You can choose a consultation using a translation app. In that case, a translation fee of 3,300 yen (tax included, not covered by insurance) is charged in addition to your insurance co-payment.

  • The translation fee is charged per visit and paid separately from the insurance co-payment.
  • Before using the app, we explain this at the reception desk and ask you to sign a consent form.
  • If you do not use a translation app, there is no translation fee. In that case, please come with an interpreter.
QHow much are medical certificates and other documents?

Certificates, documents and reissued prescriptions are not covered by health insurance, so they are charged as self-pay. All prices include tax.

  • Certificate of recovery / permission to return to school (influenza, COVID-19, etc.; elementary school age and older): 1,100 yen
  • Permission to return to nursery school / certificate of recovery (under elementary school age): free (on the nursery school's or kindergarten's own form) / 550 yen (on our clinic's form)
  • Reissue of a prescription (lost or expired): 680 yen
  • Medical certificate (our clinic's form): 3,300 yen
  • Filling in a form specified by your school or company: 2,200 yen
  • Certificate of visit / certificate of attendance: 2,200 yen
  • Influenza vaccination certificate: 1,100 yen
  • Medical certificate for a life or medical insurance company: 8,800 yen
  • Reissue of a receipt or itemized statement: 550 yen
  • English medical certificate: 11,000 yen

We do not prepare medical certificates for the physical disability certificate (shintai shogaisha techo) or disability pension, or referral information for hearing aid fitting.

A prescription is valid for 4 days, including the day of issue. Please take it to a pharmacy as soon as possible.

QWhat payment methods do you accept?

We accept cash, credit cards, e-money and transportation IC cards (including contactless payment).

QR code payments (PayPay, LINE Pay and similar) cannot be used.

QI lost my prescription, or it has expired. What should I do?

If your symptoms have not changed and you would like the same prescription reissued because it simply expired, it costs 680 yen (self-pay). If your symptoms have changed and the doctor examines you again, the usual consultation fee applies.

  • Lost prescription: reissuing is not covered by health insurance, so it costs 680 yen (self-pay).
  • Lost the paper copy of an electronic prescription: no reissue is needed. At the pharmacy, show your My Number health insurance card, or the exchange number on the copy and your insurance card.
  • We made a mistake on the prescription: we will rewrite it free of charge.
  • You cannot get to a pharmacy within 4 days because of holidays or travel: please tell the doctor during your visit.
  • A family member picks it up for you: please have them bring your patient card or insurance card.

We do not send prescriptions by fax. Please come to the clinic to collect it. Because the 680 yen reissue fee is self-pay, medical subsidy certificates (such as children's medical expense subsidies) cannot be used for it.

A prescription is valid for 4 days, including the day of issue (including Saturdays, Sundays and holidays). Please take it to a pharmacy as soon as possible.

Appointments and your visit

QDo I need an appointment? How do I book?

Appointments are required. Please book before your visit. The number of patients the doctor can see is limited, so we cannot see patients beyond the available appointment slots. If appointments are full, please choose another day or consider another medical facility. If you need urgent advice, please call the Tokyo Emergency Medical Consultation Center (#7119).

Online booking uses the booking system "Digisma" (デジスマ). If there is a free slot, you can also book for the same day.

  • If you do not have Japanese health insurance (e.g. travelers), choose the menu "Foreign visitors / 旅行者の方（自費）".
  • If you have Japanese health insurance and would like a consultation using a translation app, choose the menu "Translation app / 通訳アプリの診察（保険）".
  • The booking system is in Japanese only, and online booking requires SMS verification with a Japanese mobile phone number. We recommend asking someone who reads Japanese (your interpreter, a family member or a friend) to help you book.

QCan I have my consultation in English?

Consultations are in Japanese. If speaking Japanese is difficult for you, please come with someone who can interpret between Japanese and your language (a family member or friend is fine). Without an interpreter, we may not be able to see you.

You can also choose a consultation using a translation app. Without Japanese health insurance, the translation fee is included in the self-pay fees. With Japanese health insurance, it costs an additional 3,300 yen (tax included, not covered by insurance). See the questions under “Fees and insurance” above.

QWhat should I bring?

If you have Japanese public health insurance, please bring your My Number health insurance card (My Number Card) or certificate of eligibility (paper health insurance cards have been abolished). A My Number health insurance card stored on your smartphone can also be used. If you have them, please also bring your medication record book (okusuri techo), referral letter, test results and any medical subsidy certificates.

If you do not have Japanese public health insurance, your visit is charged in full as self-pay. If speaking Japanese is difficult for you, please come with an interpreter.

QCan children be seen?

Yes. We see children for ear, nose and throat problems, and we try to make the visit gentle so that children feel at ease.

QCan a child come without a parent?

Elementary and junior high school students must be accompanied by a parent or guardian. High school students and older can come on their own.

QCan I come just to have earwax removed?

Yes. Cleaning your ears at home can push earwax deeper or damage the eardrum. Please feel free to visit us.

QCan I ask about my medicine or symptoms by phone?

We are sorry, but we do not answer questions about consultations or medicine by phone. If you would like to talk to the doctor again or are worried about your symptoms, please book an appointment and visit us. For how to take your medicine or side effects, please ask the pharmacist at the pharmacy where you received it. If you need urgent advice, please call the Tokyo Emergency Medical Consultation Center (#7119).

QI left something at the clinic. What should I do?

Items left at the clinic are kept for 1 week from the day of your visit. Please contact us by phone (Japanese only).

Treatment and tests

QWhere do I get my medicine?

Medicine is not dispensed at our clinic. We issue a prescription, and you receive and pay for your medicine at a pharmacy. There is a pharmacy on the 1st floor of this building.

QCan I get advice about hay fever?

Yes. We also offer allergy tests and advice on sublingual immunotherapy, which may improve your allergy at its root.

QDo you do allergy tests?

Yes. We do allergy tests with a blood sample. Please note that we do not take blood samples from children.

QIs an endoscope (fiberscope) examination always necessary?

Depending on your symptoms, we may use an endoscope to check your nose and throat in detail. If you are worried about pain or cost, please tell the doctor during your visit.

QI have had a cough for a long time. Which department should I see?

If your cough has lasted 2 weeks or more, or is so bad that you cannot sleep at night, we recommend seeing a respiratory medicine department. If the cough comes with nose or throat symptoms, such as a runny or stuffy nose or throat discomfort, an ENT examination is often helpful, and we can see you. After the examination, we may refer you to internal (respiratory) medicine.

QAre there tests or services you do not provide?

We are sorry, but we do not provide the following:

  • Eustachian tube inflation or treatment (for patulous or obstructed Eustachian tube, etc.)
  • Acoustic (stapedial) reflex tests
  • Exercise therapy for BPPV (benign paroxysmal positional vertigo)
  • Applications or renewals of the physical disability certificate (shintai shogaisha techo)
  • Medical expense subsidies for designated intractable diseases
  • Treatment under compulsory automobile liability insurance (traffic accidents), workers' accident compensation insurance, or medical assistance under public assistance (seikatsu hogo)

If you need any of these, please consider another medical facility.
