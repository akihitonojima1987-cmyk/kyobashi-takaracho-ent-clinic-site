What is epipharyngitis?

The epipharynx is the uppermost part of the throat, behind the nose. Air passes through it, and it contains a lot of tissue involved in the immune system. When inflammation continues there for a long time, it is called chronic epipharyngitis.

It is thought to be caused by ongoing irritation: inflammation left after a cold or viral infection, nasal discharge from allergic rhinitis or sinusitis, dryness from mouth breathing, or acid reflux. Besides throat discomfort and postnasal drip, it may also be linked to general symptoms such as headaches and fatigue.

What is B-spot therapy?

In B-spot therapy, a cotton swab soaked in zinc chloride solution is rubbed directly on the epipharynx, through the nose or the mouth. It is also called EAT (Epipharyngeal Abrasive Therapy) and is used for chronic epipharyngitis. It is a simple treatment, and its effectiveness for chronic epipharyngitis has been reported.

Because it is important to treat the inflamed area thoroughly, we check the epipharynx with a thin nasal endoscope when needed. Depending on your condition, the treatment may be done through the mouth.

How it works

1. Anti-inflammatory

Zinc chloride is thought to tighten the mucous membrane and calm inflammation.

2. Drainage

The slight bleeding caused by rubbing is expected to help remove substances involved in inflammation.

3. Autonomic nerves

Stimulating the epipharynx is thought to affect the whole body through the vagus nerve and other nerves.

* How the treatment works is still being studied, and there are several theories. Results vary from person to person.

The procedure

1

Consultation

The doctor asks about your symptoms and checks your epipharynx. Please tell us if you would like B-spot therapy.

2

Local anesthesia (if needed)

A spray anesthetic is applied inside the nose. Please tell us in advance if you are allergic to anesthetics.

3

Checking the epipharynx

When needed, we look at the epipharynx with a thin endoscope. Depending on your condition, the treatment may be done through the mouth.

4

Treatment

The epipharynx is rubbed with a swab soaked in zinc chloride. The procedure itself takes only a short time.

Symptoms it may help

It may help with the following symptoms when they are thought to be related to chronic epipharyngitis.

Postnasal dripThroat discomfort or painCough and phlegmHoarsenessHeadacheStiff shoulders, fatigueEar symptoms (ringing, fullness)Feeling unwell after a cold or infection

* Results vary greatly from person to person, and the treatment may not be effective enough for some patients. Postnasal drip in particular is the most common complaint but often takes time to improve. The doctor decides whether the treatment is suitable for you after an examination.

Number of sessions

It depends on how severe your symptoms are and how long they have lasted. As a guide, we do about 10 to 15 sessions at once or twice a week, and then judge how well it is working. If it helps, we continue. If it does not help much, we do not recommend continuing.

Once your symptoms settle down, we space out the sessions and see how you do. Depending on your condition, we may continue at longer intervals to prevent a relapse.

Precautions and side effects

  • This treatment does not work for every symptom. Results vary from person to person.
  • After the treatment, your throat may sting for a few hours to a few days. Pain and bleeding tend to be stronger when the inflammation is severe, and usually ease as you continue the sessions.
  • You may have slight bleeding or more nasal discharge or phlegm for a while after the treatment. This is not a cause for concern.
  • In rare cases, effects of zinc chloride on the sense of smell have been reported.
  • The treatment requires visits once or twice a week.
  • There are no special restrictions on eating or bathing after the treatment. It can also be done during pregnancy or breastfeeding.

The treatment may be difficult if you have recently had an asthma attack or take several blood-thinning medicines. The doctor will decide after an examination, so please talk to us.

Self-care at home

Nasal rinsing

Washes away pollen, dust and viruses from the back of the nose and throat. Over-the-counter kits make it easy.

Breathe through your nose

Mouth breathing dries the epipharynx and causes inflammation. If your mouth opens while you sleep, mouth tape can help.

Keep your neck warm

Warming the back of the neck to improve blood flow is thought to help epipharyngitis get better.

Frequently asked questions

QDoes it hurt? How long does the pain last?

Some pain is expected, because a swab is inserted through the nose or mouth and rubbed on the epipharynx. The stronger the inflammation, the more it tends to sting, but it eases as the sessions continue and the inflammation settles. In most cases, the pain becomes mild within a few hours.

QFrom what age can children have it?

There is no strict age limit, but the child needs to be able to tolerate the discomfort of the procedure. As a guide, we consider it from the upper grades of elementary school (around age 10). Please talk to the doctor at your visit.

QIs it covered by insurance?

Yes, it is covered by Japanese health insurance. Without Japanese health insurance, you pay the full cost yourself. See Cost above (for example, 7,700 yen for a follow-up treatment-only visit within 1 month of the previous one).

QCan I have it during pregnancy or while breastfeeding?

Yes. If you have any concerns, please tell the doctor at your consultation.
